The Fool

The fool in our name means two things, and we want both.

The first fool is foolish enough to say what he’s never said. He risks being wrong, being seen and being disagreed with. He says it anyway, because the conversation you’ve been avoiding is usually the one that matters most.

The second fool is foolish enough to listen to everything and hold back judgment. He lets you finish. He wants to know where you’re coming from. When he disagrees, he challenges the belief, never the person.

The wise stay quiet to keep looking wise. The fool finally hears what it sounds like out loud.

Together they add up to humility. A wise man knows he is a fool. Socrates told the court that tried him he was wiser than other men only because “I neither know nor think that I know.” Montaigne had a medal struck with the question “What do I know?”

That’s what we ask of everyone in the room. Hold your beliefs firmly enough to say them out loud, and loosely enough to let someone else sharpen them. As fools, we seek to understand, and we don’t cross-examine anyone to win anything but our own understanding.

And when you step into your truth, you own it. You don’t get to run from it when it turns inconvenient.

In the tarot, the Fool is the card with no number, or the zero: the one at the edge of the cliff, at the very start of the journey. Every important conversation starts there.
